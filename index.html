Boardwalk

Boardwalk turns your M-VAVE FM-1 into a seaside arcade of music games. You walk along the boardwalk at golden hour, with the sea and the gulls behind you, and each cabinet plays its own little tune. Step up to one to play. You can ring the changes on a tower's bells, name a note by ear, play a song on falling tiles, fly a gull through a pier's posts, or ride a roller coaster whose track is the melody. Every game pays out tickets. Trade them at the prize counter at the start of the row for anything from a sticker to a giant teddy.

This is version 0.9.2. Installing is at your own risk, and the installer can put M-VAVE's own firmware back.

  • Try it in the browser, with sound. It runs the same code as the FM-1, so you don't need one.
  • Install it from Chrome or Edge, with the FM-1 connected by USB.
  • Download boardwalk-0.9.2.fwsc to use the command-line installer: python3 base/tools/fm1_install.py boardwalk-0.9.2.fwsc.
  • Source is under MPL-2.0. It's built on fm1-base and felucca-bsp, from Felucca by Leo Kuroshita, Hügelton Instruments.

Playing

On the boardwalk, press OCT− / OCT+ (or turn SELECT) to walk along the cabinets, and press PLAY to step up to the one in front of you. In a game, press PLAY to start and stop a round, and play it on the keys. Turn KNOB 1 to pick the game's level or mode, and press HOME to step back onto the boardwalk. On the boardwalk itself, KNOB 1 sets the sound of the sea and KNOB 2 the cabinets' tunes.

Each game remembers your best. When you finish a round, it pays tickets, and you get more for playing well and on harder levels. As you leave, the cabinet feeds them out of its slot. The prizes you trade for sit on the boardwalk's rail, and two of them change the boardwalk itself.

Boardwalk also tells the FM-1 Screen Reader what its screen shows, over USB.

To go back to the official firmware, use "Back to the stock firmware" in the installer, or M-VAVE's own updater, M-UPGRADE, from m-vave.com/download.

Change ringing's methods are written from the Central Council of Church Bell Ringers' collection. The songs are traditional or long out of copyright. Some cabinets are inspired by classic games (Simon, Flappy Bird, Piano Tiles, Whac-A-Mole), but none of their code, art or names is used.
